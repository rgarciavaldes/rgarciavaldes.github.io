RGV

Raúl García-Valdés

Associate Professor (Profesor Titular) · Universidad Rey Juan Carlos · Global Change Research Institute (IICG-URJC), Madrid

I study how climate change alters the composition, diversity and functioning of forests. I combine national forest inventories, functional-trait databases and satellite time series with niche and process-based models, from regional to continental scales, to understand and predict the stability and resilience of European forests under increasing drought.

Research

Research lines

Why do some forests withstand drought and others decline? My work links what forests are made of (species, traits, structure) to how they function and respond to climate.

Species composition and forest responses to climate change

Long-term climate-change impacts on forest productivity are driven mainly by species turnover rather than by changes in tree growth, and climate-driven extinctions affect forest functioning more than random extinctions.

Glob Ecol Biogeogr 2020 · Divers Distrib 2018 · Sci Rep 2018

Functional diversity, stability and resilience

Diversity in drought-tolerance and hydraulic traits compensates the productivity cost of drought-tolerant communities and increases the temporal stability of forests across thousands of inventory plots.

Glob Change Biol 2021 · For Ecol Manage 2025 · Eur J For Res 2026

Trait-based prediction of drought impacts

International syntheses on hydraulic risk, drought-induced mortality and range shifts, and forest dynamics models (MEDFATE) used for scenario simulation.

Nat Ecol Evol 2023, 2025 · New Phytol 2023 · Geosci Model Dev 2023

Events

Past events

11–13 December 2024 · URJC, Móstoles

Workshop on the effects of tree trait diversity on forest stability and resilience

Funded by the IICG-URJC Forward Thinking Actions (1st call) and organised by R. García-Valdés and A. I. García-Cervigón. Eighteen researchers from Spain, Finland, Germany, the UK and the USA, including CREAF, University of Cambridge, University of Connecticut, University of Jyväskylä and TU Munich, spent two and a half days in ten working sessions on forest stability, functional traits, national forest inventories and remote sensing.

  • Launched the DivTraitFor network, with regular online meetings and shared data.
  • Started a European-scale paper on tree trait diversity and forest stability using remote-sensing metrics.
  • Identified seven spin-off research ideas and new funding opportunities, and began a collaboration with the TreeMort database.

2025 · Pontevedra

Talk at the III SIBECOL & XVII AEET Meeting

Does diversity in drought-tolerance traits promote temporal stability in forest productivity? R. García-Valdés, J. V. Roces-Díaz, M. De Cáceres, et al., J. Martínez-Vilalta.

Water availability was the strongest driver of the stability of forest productivity across Spain, followed by stand structure and drought-tolerance diversity (published in Forest Ecology and Management, 2025).
